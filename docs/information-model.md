Information Model

Purpose

This document defines how information is categorized and managed in the Disability Law Platform.

The platform separates publicly available legal information from restricted advocacy knowledge to protect strategic information while preserving collective knowledge.

⸻

Information Layers

Layer 1. Public Legal Knowledge

Information that can be accessed publicly.

Examples:

* Laws
* Enforcement Decrees
* Enforcement Rules
* Administrative regulations
* International conventions
* UN CRPD
* General Comments
* Public court decisions
* Public government reports

Characteristics:

* Official source required
* Source citation required
* Version and amendment history maintained

⸻

Layer 2. Organization Knowledge

Information available only to authorized members.

Examples:

* Internal legal analysis
* Advocacy strategies
* Negotiation materials
* Campaign planning documents
* Policy proposals before publication
* Field experience notes

Characteristics:

* Access controlled
* Contributor recorded
* Revision history maintained

⸻

Layer 3. Personal Knowledge

Information managed by individual users.

Examples:

* Personal research notes
* Draft ideas
* Private annotations
* Unpublished thoughts

Characteristics:

* Private by default
* User-controlled sharing

⸻

Data Relationships

The platform connects information layers.

Example:

Legal Article
      |
      |
Disability Rights Issue
      |
      |
Advocacy Knowledge
      |
      |
Personal Notes

Public legal sources provide the foundation, while organizational and personal knowledge add practical experience.

⸻

Core Principle

The platform preserves collective knowledge without exposing sensitive advocacy strategies.

Information accessibility must always be considered together with the purpose and context of the information.
